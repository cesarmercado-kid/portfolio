What the file supports, measured across all 1,338 policies

The variable that sets the price Smoking status

Smokers pay $32,050 on average against $8,434 for non-smokers, 280% more. No other variable in the file produces a difference of comparable size, so a rating structure that does not separate the two groups first will misprice both.

Where the cost compounds Smokers above a BMI of 30

Among smokers, charges climb sharply from the obesity threshold; among non-smokers, BMI barely moves the premium. The two variables interact, they do not add up as separate loadings. Mean BMI among smokers is 30.71, already at that threshold.

How to read the regional gap As portfolio composition

The Southeast shows the highest mean, the widest dispersion, and both the minimum and the maximum of the file. It also concentrates 33% of all smokers, with 25% of its insured smoking. The region is expensive because of who it covers.

Age

Within the interquartile range of smokers the correlation between age and charges comes out negative. The cause is regulatory rather than statistical: policyholders over 65 move to Medicare, which truncates the private-market curve. Age should not be taken from this file as a standalone rating factor.
