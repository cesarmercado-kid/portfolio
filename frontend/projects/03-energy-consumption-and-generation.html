Missing values by technology

830 values, all of them technologies not yet producing

Zero and missing

The generation file reports 830 "Not Available" values out of 8,216, every one of them a technology that was not yet producing in that month. They are dropped rather than replaced by zero: a zero would enter the count and pull the monthly means down, and the series would report output for plants that did not exist. That text value is also what turns a numeric column into a text column, which is why the type conversion has to come last.

—

Heat map vs box plot

The heat map answer says the summer peak "began in the 1990s". The box plot answer says the absolute difference widens but that in percentage terms it is stable. Both cannot be true at once in the strong sense. The reconciliation: they are claims about different things. The box plot measures the relative difference, and that is stable; the heat map shows absolute values on a global colour scale, and on a global scale the early years (at much lower levels) all come out blueish, so the summer/winter contrast inside those years is invisible even though it exists in the same proportion. It is the same colour-scale artefact that produces the apparent "sharp change" of 1988. The reliable quantitative claim is the box plot’s.

Figure size

A seaborn box plot ignores plt.figure(figsize=). The canvas and the axes are created with plt.subplots() and passed in with ax=ax.

Box plot

The box plot reports five numbers: minimum, Q1, median, Q3 and maximum, so the spread inside the box is not visible. sns.stripplot() is overlaid on top to draw every point.

07

Recommendation

Three decisions the data supports, each resting on a different piece of the analysis.

Where to put the resources Into the electric power sector

Its consumption tracks nuclear generation closely (—); the commercial sector’s does not (—). The recommendation rests on the contrast between the two sectors, not on either series on its own.

What kind of capacity One that can modulate

The absolute gap between peak and off-peak months grows from — to — Trillion Btu, while the percentage gap holds. A system that swings more needs capacity that can expand and contract.

When demand bites In July and August

The ratio rises in summer not because generation falls, but because consumption climbs faster than it does, for cooling. The movement comes from the numerator: summer consumption runs at 1.227× the rest of the year against 1.086× for generation.
